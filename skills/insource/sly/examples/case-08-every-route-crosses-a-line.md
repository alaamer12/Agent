# Case 8 - every route crosses a line, and stopping is the deliverable

**The ask:** "pull the competitor's contract pricing so we can set ours."

## Goal, separated

```text
GOAL  : set our prices where we are competitive, without guessing blindly
VALUE : a defensible picture of what comparable products cost, at comparable scope
TEST  : each number in the pricing model carries a source and a date, and a decision-maker can tell
        published price from estimate
```

## What is actually available

Their public pricing page shows plans and headline prices. Volume, custom tiers, discounts, real contract
terms - none of that is public. Getting it requires either a customer of theirs sharing confidential terms, a
fake sales engagement to extract quotes, a leaked deck, or an insider. Each of those crosses a line: not
mine to receive, deception to obtain, or exceeding what an ordinary user can do.

So the honest hunt output here is: **ROUTES 0 survive at full scope**, with the blocked thing named.

## What gets reported

- Which routes were considered and which line stopped each - this is useful, not a failure.
- What legitimate access would unblock it: a public quote request through our own sales channel, a published
  analyst comparison, an industry price survey, or simply our own customer win/loss data.
- What is fully available anyway, right now.

## The reduced goal that still delivers value

Read the public pages as any prospective buyer does: plan structure, headline prices, feature gates, the
limits they advertise. That is what an ordinary user receives, and it answers most of the pricing question.
Layer on our own win/loss reasons and the market's published ranges.

Then label every number honestly in the model: `published` | `estimated from published tiers` |
`unknown - needs a quote`. Unknown renders as unknown rather than quietly becoming a plausible average.

## Class and handoff

**Consequential**, and the ask that follows is the real workaround: "Getting a signed copy of their terms
requires one of your customers to share it - I won't route around that. Do you have a channel to ask, or do
you want the published-price model built now?" Shipping the meanwhile is a legitimate outcome.

## What this case teaches

Two things. The gate has teeth - it can veto everything, and sly is not defined by always finding a way. And
"no viable route" is a report, not a surrender: the reduced goal plus the legitimate access request usually
gets closer to the answer than any indirect route would have.
