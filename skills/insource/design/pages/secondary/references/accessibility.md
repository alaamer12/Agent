# Accessibility statement

The conformance claim - therefore the honesty document: it states a target, lists what does NOT
yet meet it, and opens the feedback channel. An empty-confidence statement ("we are fully
accessible") without an audit behind it is the one failure mode this page exists to avoid.

## Must include

1. **The target standard, cited** - [WCAG 2.2 AA] is the common contract; the statement names it
   and the date the product measured itself against it. EU-consumers-facing services under the
   **European Accessibility Act (enforceable since 28 Jun 2025: e-commerce, banking, payments,
   transport, telecoms, e-books)** reference **EN 301 549** (v3.2.1 = WCAG 2.1 AA; v4.1.1 =
   WCAG 2.2 AA since Sep 2026) and must exist at all — microenterprises (<10 staff, ≤€2M) are
   exempt for services only.
2. **Constatus, honestly graded:**
   - *compliant* - verified how (self-check pass, [third-party audit] named if it exists),
   - *partial* - which areas fail,
   - *not applicable / out of scope* - third-party embeds with their own limitation named.
3. **Known limitations** - a real list with the workaround where one exists; "none" only after an
   actual audit; where a requirement is skipped as disproportionate, the **documented burden
   argument** (EAA Art. 14 / Annex VI) stands in the open, revisited per law.
4. **Feedback + alternative-format channel** - the human route and the response commitment (ASK
   for the real window); under EAA this must itself be accessible.
5. **The complaint route** - the national EAA authority (or ADA/section-504 frame in the US)
   named, so the statement connects to enforcement rather than pretending it away.
6. **The improving plan** - dated, linked to real work items; a plan with no dates is vibes.
7. **Methodology** - what tools/tests ran (auto-checker + manual keyboard/screen-reader passes
   stated truthfully).

## Writing professionally

- Plain language, first paragraph = "we target WCAG 2.2 AA; here is where we are and how to tell
  us about a barrier."
- Screen-reader parity applies to the statement itself: semantic headings, alt on its images,
  focus order - a non-accessible accessibility page is the loudest irony in this catalog.
- Jurisdictions differ (EU public-sector directives, US ADA case law); which frames apply is an
  ASK, and the page says "for our users in X" not "worldwide compliant."

## Ask the user - never invent

Actual audit status; the real known-gaps list; the feedback owner and window; dates; which legal
frames matter for the product's market.

## Failure patterns

A widget/vendor badge pasted as the whole strategy (overlay tools are a documented failure class,
not a conformance story); claims dated years ago; "contact us" with an unmonitored address (the
statement becomes a complaint trap - `contact.md` rules apply); an undated "fully compliant" text
while US Title III web suits ran 3,000+ in 2025 (+27%, online-only retailers included) and EAA
enforcement produced first court orders mid-2026.
