# Terms of Sale

The per-purchase contract: order, price, delivery, risk — sitting under the account ToS. Needed by
anyone selling goods (or one-time digital purchases) directly to consumers from their own site;
SaaS-only and store-only sellers skip it (their terms live in the subscription ToS and the store's
developer agreement).

## Must include

1. **Formation, stated** — the listing is an invitation to treat; the contract forms at *ship* or
   at a written acceptance, not at "Order Confirmed" — auto-acceptance means a mispriced order
   binds you (the Amazon/Best Buy pattern: acknowledge → accept when dispatched).
2. **Price, tax, duties** + a **manifest-error clause** — the right to cancel obviously mispriced
   orders with refund; state it or lose it.
3. **Delivery promises that the fulfillment stack can keep** — US: the FTC Mail/Internet/Telephone
   Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR 435): ship within the stated time or 30 days, else offer consent
   to delay or prompt refund; EU: pre-contract info duties (CRD Arts. 5-8) incl. delivery
   arrangements. Lead-time vs delivery-time split lives with `shipping-policy.md` — one owner.
4. **Risk of loss / title transfer** — shipment vs destination contract (UCC 2-509) in plain
   words; consumers care when the parcel dies at the door.
5. **Cancellation/withdrawal cross-ref** — the exit mechanics belong to `cancellation-withdrawal.md`;
   this page links, never restates; "all sales final" contradicted by a statutory withdrawal right
   is the classic internal lie.
6. **Discount-price honesty** [EU] — "was/now" claims reference the lowest price of the prior
   30 days (CJEU C-330/23, 2024).
7. **Precedence line** — ToS → these Terms of Sale → order confirmation; each doc names the other
   instead of duplicating.

## Writing professionally

- Buyers skim for three numbers: when it ships, what it costs all-in, how to get out. Answer those
  in the top quarter of the page.
- Every promise cross-checked against the real cart/email stack — a formation clause the checkout
  does not implement is worse than silence.

## Ask the user - never invent

Acceptance trigger in their stack; misprice handling; ship-time capability per zone; whether
consumer distance sales into EU/UK happen (withdrawal applies); who bears return shipping (cross-ref
warranty/returns).

## Failure patterns

A 30-page ToS silent on delivery and risk; auto-"we accept your order" + binding mispriced stock;
hidden fees caught by EU/AU enforcement; no precedence rule so ToS and sale terms contradict on
cancellation; US-only refund language shown to EU customers.
