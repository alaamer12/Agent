# Ultra's judgment calls, and when to surface them

Two failure modes pull in opposite directions: silently defaulting to the least-changed option, and silently deciding a consequential call on the person's behalf.

### Ultra pushes for maximum accessibility, but consults on judgment calls that affect it

Ultra's mandate is to push readability and calm as far as they genuinely go, not to settle for whatever change is smallest or safest-looking. Several individual moves at Ultra involve a real judgment call with more than one defensible answer — the working-surface light/dark direction (palette move above) is one, chrome's position and chrome's color (side/top/bottom/floating; dark-neutral, light-neutral, or full brand-hue) are others, and the same applies to other Ultra-only decisions with a comparably large effect on the result (e.g. how aggressively to collapse pages, how far to push a from-scratch visual language versus staying close to the original's specific look). Two failure modes to avoid here, and they pull in opposite directions:

- **Silently defaulting to the least-changed option** because it's the safe-looking middle ground (e.g. "keep it dark, just softer" without ever seriously evaluating light) — Ultra explicitly does not reward staying close to the original when a bigger change would genuinely read better and more accessible.
- **Silently deciding a big, consequential judgment call on the person's behalf** without surfacing it, when the person would reasonably want a say (a fundamentally different visual direction, not just an execution detail).

Resolve this by: forming a genuine recommendation from what the input itself signals (a dark-context media/creative tool's own before-state is real evidence it should probably stay dark; a dense all-dark admin/data console is real evidence its working surface would likely read better breaking away from dark, since dark-everywhere in that kind of tool is usually an unexamined default, not an intentional identity choice) — then, for the judgment calls with the largest effect on the outcome, state the recommendation and proceed with it as the default, while explicitly inviting a quick correction if the person has a different preference, rather than either asking a blocking question or deciding silently with no visibility into the call that was made.

The same treatment applies to **how large the type/spacing scale itself goes** (see the typography move below for the comfortable/large/enormous spectrum) — at Ultra this compounds with every other move (a bigger type scale plus a multi-page split plus a rebuilt palette is a much bigger combined change than any one alone), so it's one of the judgment calls to name explicitly rather than default through silently.
