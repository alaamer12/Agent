# Wasit (وسيط) / Mutawassit (متوسط) — project idea

*(Working title — the name is not settled yet. See Open questions.)*

## The idea in one line

A free phone app where Muslim writers publish articles and readers follow the writers they trust — the plain blogging core of Medium, aimed at Islamic writing, with no paywall and no money anywhere in it.

## Ancestry

| Ancestor | Relationship | What the project takes | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | instance of | The core blogging model carried over almost intact: anyone can write an article and publish it, readers follow the writers they like, and what those writers publish comes to them; a clean reading experience built for long text | Its business — the membership/paywall model, the writer payouts, and the "profit" the prompt explicitly rules out; its branded multi-writer publications and editor-curated front page; and its advanced story-presentation and formatting features, which the prompt names as things this project will not have |

**In one paragraph:** Medium's publishing-and-following core, applied to a different world — Islamic writing and its readers — which is what makes this an *instance* of Medium rather than a loose inspiration. Everything else about Medium is refused whole: no money changes hands anywhere (no paywall, no membership, no writer earnings), and none of the presentation machinery that makes a Medium story feel "designed". No other ancestor was named, so being phone-only, being free forever, and the Islamic focus itself are this project's own choices rather than inheritances — and the project's own invention is exactly that combination: Medium's basics, stripped of its economy, in the pocket, for this audience.

## What it is

Medium is a well-known website where anyone can publish a long article and where readers follow the writers they like. This project is that idea, much smaller and on a phone: you open the app, you write an article, you publish it, and people who follow you see it. Readers follow the writers whose voice they trust — a teacher, a student of knowledge, a friend who writes well — and their new articles arrive in one place instead of being scattered across social media.

It is built around Islamic writing: articles about the religion, its texts, its practice, its life. But the app itself is deliberately plain. There is no membership, no paid section, no subscription, no advertising, and no writer earnings. Every article is open to every reader, because there is no money in the product at all.

## Who it serves

Muslims who write about their religion and Muslims who want to read about it properly.

On the writing side: people with something to say and no good place to say it at length — teachers, imams, students of knowledge, and ordinary people who write thoughtfully. Today their long pieces end up as Facebook posts, WhatsApp forwards, or messages in a Telegram channel: places built for short bursts, where a serious article scrolls away in a day and cannot be found again. The other option, a general blogging site like Medium, works technically but is not their room — its audience, its tone, and its paywall are someone else's.

On the reading side: people who want more than a caption or a clip, who prefer to read something whole, and who want to keep up with a handful of writers they already respect rather than with an endless feed chosen by a machine.

## What it includes

**Write and publish.** Anyone with an account can write an article and publish it from the phone, in a few steps. Each writer has a simple page that lists what they have written, so a reader who likes one article can find the rest.

**Read.** Articles open in a clean, comfortable phone reading view. Reading is the centre of the product; everything else exists to serve it.

**Follow.** A reader can follow any writer. The new articles from the writers that reader follows arrive together in one stream — a feed, a single place to check — so keeping up with ten writers does not mean visiting ten places.

**Be free.** No subscription, no membership, no paywall, no advertising, no paid tier, no writer earnings. Every article is readable by everyone, always. Free is not a pricing strategy here; it is part of what the product is.

That is the whole product as described: write, read, follow, free. Anything beyond those four — searching, liking, commenting, editing depth — was not stated, and is listed under Open questions rather than assumed.

## What it does not include

**Ruled out by the user:**

- Any form of earning: no subscriptions, no paywall, no "premium" articles, no advertising, no writer payouts, no donations mechanism. The user's words were "no profit or anything like Medium — it's completely free".
- Medium's deep feature set: the story-presentation and formatting features the prompt names, and by extension the long tail of Medium extras. This is intentionally a mini product, not a smaller copy of everything.
- The "everything Medium has" scope in general. If a feature is not part of writing, reading, following, or being free, it is not in this idea.

**Tempting Medium features this project refuses:**

- The membership/paywall business and the writer-earnings programme — the single most tempting thing to copy from Medium, and the one thing explicitly refused.
- Branded group publications (shared spaces where several writers post under one masthead) and an editor-curated front page: the described idea reaches readers through following, not through an editorial or algorithmic layer.
- The heavy presentation layer — elaborate layout and formatting controls — in favour of plain, readable articles.

Where a Medium feature is neither stated nor implied by the user's words — likes, comments, saving an article, search — it is not decided here; it sits in Open questions.

## Open questions

1. **The name.** Wasit (وسيط) or Mutawassit (متوسط)? Both were offered as "something like that", and the user said the name "won't be big anyway". Related: does the "in-between / middle" meaning say something about the product's positioning, or is it only a word that sounds right?
2. **Platforms.** iOS, Android, or both? Is there any way to read an article outside the app — for example, opening a shared link in a browser on a computer — or is the phone app the only door?
3. **Language.** Arabic only, English only, or both? If both, is a single article written in one language, or can it exist in more than one? Arabic also raises right-to-left reading and typography, which the app would have to handle properly.
4. **Beyond following, what social basics exist?** Liking or appreciating an article, commenting, replying, saving something to read later, sharing a link — which of these are part of "the basics of blogging" for this project, and which are the "deep features" to leave out?
5. **How plain is the editor?** Plain text only, or bold and headings, quotes, images, links? The line between "basic blogging" and the "story presentation" the user rejected has not been drawn.
6. **Editorial position.** Is everything published instantly and unmoderated, or is there review? Who decides what counts as fitting Islamic content, and are articles outside that scope allowed at all? This changes what the product is, not just how it is run.
7. **Discovery beyond following.** Can a reader find writers they do not already know — by topic, by search, by a curated list — or is following the only way in? The prompt named following and nothing else.
8. **Reading without an account.** Can someone read freely before signing up, or is an account required to read as well as to write?
9. **Who runs it and who pays for it.** If there is no revenue of any kind, someone still pays for hosting and upkeep, and someone is responsible for the app's content and behavior. Who, and is that a person, a small team, a charity, or a community?
