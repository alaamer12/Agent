# The final report - ten sections and eight verdict questions

Reveal's output is a structured investigation report, written from the evidence ledger, not from memory or
from the researcher outputs concatenated. The fixed shape is in `assets/report-template.md`; this file is why
each section exists and what makes it honest.

## The ten sections

1. **Executive Summary** - the whole finding in a page: overall uniqueness assessment, how many relevant
   competitors (by class), the most important discoveries, the main competitive threats, the biggest genuine
   differentiation opportunities. Written last, placed first. If the user reads nothing else, this must not
   mislead - the uncomfortable finding leads, it is not buried.
2. **Project Breakdown** - the Phase 1 brief restated: the idea, features, audience, model, markets. This is
   what everything was measured *against*; if it is wrong, the whole report is wrong, so it is shown for the
   user to correct.
3. **Competitive Landscape** - the discovered set as a table: name, website, description, country of origin,
   main markets, establishment date, target audience, overall similarity score, class (direct/indirect/
   alternative/adjacent). The map of the field.
4. **Competitor Deep Dives** - the ten-dimension investigation of the most relevant competitors (the high-
   similarity and high-threat ones), each claim traceable to the ledger. Not all candidates get a deep dive;
   the ones that move the verdict do.
5. **Feature Comparison Matrix** - proposed project vs the closest competitors, feature by feature. The
   functional truth of "how much already exists," in one view.
6. **Geographical & Language Coverage** - where each competitor operates, which languages/RTL/local payments
   it supports, and which markets and language groups remain **underserved**. This section is where a regional
   or localization wedge becomes visible as evidence rather than hope.
7. **Market & User Analysis** - available user/popularity statistics, business models, growth and viability
   evidence, compared. Every number labeled fact/estimate/assumption with its method; proxies never presented
   as active users.
8. **Uniqueness Scorecard** - the eight lenses ([`05`](05-uniqueness-assessment.md)) with a per-lens verdict
   and the evidence behind it, and the synthesis that holds both "what exists" and "what is open" at once.
9. **Competitive Gaps & Opportunities** - the actionable openings, each grounded in evidence: features no
   competitor has, audiences poorly served, countries/locales underserved, localization gaps, recurring user
   complaints against incumbents, missing integrations. An "opportunity" that is not tied to a competitor's
   demonstrated weakness or absence is labeled as the user's hypothesis, not a finding.
10. **Final Verdict** - the eight questions answered directly, in prose, with the evidence pointed to.

## The eight verdict questions - answered, not hedged

The report ends by answering these plainly:

1. Is this idea already implemented? *(where, and how closely)*
2. Which existing project is most similar? *(name, score, why)*
3. How much of the proposed functionality already exists? *(from the feature matrix, not a vibe)*
4. What makes the proposed project different? *(the differences the evidence supports - and the ones it does
   not)*
5. Is there a meaningful competitive advantage? *(and if the honest answer is "not yet visible," say that)*
6. Is the project entering an overcrowded market? *(count by class; crowded is not fatal - see the two
   confusions)*
7. Are there underserved markets worth targeting? *(the geographical/language/audience gaps actually found)*
8. What should change to make the project more distinctive? *(concrete, evidence-derived - what to add, drop,
   or reposition, given competitor weaknesses and absences)*

## What makes the report trustworthy

- **It is willing to disappoint.** A verdict that finds heavy competition and no clear edge says so. The
  skill's value is proportional to its honesty about bad news.
- **Every claim is checkable.** URLs and dates live in the ledger; the report cites competitor names and
  points back. The ledger passes the defect audit in [`06`](06-research-quality-and-evidence.md) before the
  report is called done: no unlabeled statistic, no estimate without a method, no score without reasoning.
- **Estimates are estimates.** Qualified, method-shown, never dressed as verified numbers.
- **Limits are stated.** A final subsection names what could not be reached (login-walled communities,
  regional stores unavailable, paid databases not used) and what that might hide - so the user knows the edge
  of the map.
- **It informs, it does not decide.** Reveal reports where the project stands; whether to build, and how to
  differentiate, is the user's call, made with the picture in front of them.
