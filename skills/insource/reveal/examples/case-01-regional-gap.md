# Case 1 - not unique anywhere on earth, but wide open in the target market

**Idea put to Reveal:** "An app that connects homeowners with vetted home-repair technicians, with upfront
fixed quotes and escrow payment." Target: Egypt and the Gulf, Arabic-first.
**Fundamental question:** how much of this already exists, and what makes ours different?

This case exists to show the shape of the honest answer when the two truths are both real: **the idea is not
original at all** (dimension after dimension already built) **and it may still be a genuine opportunity**
(the specific market is underserved). Reveal reports both without letting either cancel the other.

## Phase 1 - the idea broken into axes (not one query)

Concept ("on-demand home services marketplace"), each problem separately (find a trusted tech / get a fair
fixed price / pay safely / guarantee the work), features (geo-matching, vetting, fixed-quote flow, escrow,
reviews), audience (homeowners, not businesses), model (marketplace take-rate), market (Egypt + GCC),
language (Arabic + RTL), platform (mobile). Nine axes; the pitch sentence would have been one.

## Phase 2 - discovery, and what the broad pass turned up

- English concept-axis on Google/Product Hunt/AlternativeTo → the famous ones (a US leader, two European).
- **Problem-axis in Arabic** on native web + Google Play KSA/EG → three regional players the English search
  never surfaced. This is the whole point of multi-language discovery: the *actual* competitors for an
  Egypt/Gulf idea were invisible to the English pass.
- "alternatives to <US leader>" rails → two more, adjacent.
- Wayback + "shut down" search → one Egyptian attempt, dead since 2020 (prior art *and* a cautionary signal -
  its post-mortem thread named unit-economics, not demand, as the killer).

Frontier rounds 4 and 5 (German feature search, Turkish directories) added nothing → stopped.

## Phase 3-4 - dimensions and similarity, where the honesty lives

The US leader scored **71 overall**: idea 90, problem 88, features 80, model 75 - but **market 20** (does not
operate in MENA), **language 10** (no Arabic, no RTL), **local payments 0** (no Meeza/stc pay, no cash-on-
delivery culture fit). The reasoning line: *"near-identical product, wrong continent - high functional, low
market similarity; a 90 would be dishonest."* That one sentence is the difference between an investigation and
a feature-list comparison.

The regional Arabic players scored **80-88** - same idea, same market, same language - and here the deep dives
found the opening: all three scored low on dimension **I (positioning)** because their reviews were dominated
by *the same two complaints*: no real vetting ("anyone can sign up") and quotes that inflate after the visit.
Dimension **J**: the two best-funded ones had flat download brackets for four quarters.

## The verdict, in the shape the report gives it

- **Already implemented?** Yes, thoroughly - the concept is a solved, crowded category globally.
- **Most similar?** A regional Arabic leader, 88 - same everything.
- **How much functionality exists?** ~all of it; nothing in the feature matrix is new.
- **What makes ours different?** The *only* evidence-backed differences: escrow + enforced fixed-quote (which
  attacks the incumbents' #1 and #2 complaints directly), and a vetting guarantee none of them actually
  enforces.
- **Meaningful advantage?** Not from the idea - the idea is common. Conditionally, from *execution against a
  proven, specific pain*: the two complaints are documented in the incumbents' own reviews, not guessed.
- **Overcrowded?** Globally yes; in-market moderately (three live, one dead).
- **Underserved worth targeting?** The trust/escrow gap in the Gulf is real and evidenced; the Egyptian
  attempt's death on unit economics is a stated risk, not hidden.
- **What should change?** Drop the "we invented marketplace booking" framing (we did not); lead on
  guaranteed-vetted + fixed-price-or-free, because that is where every incumbent is demonstrably weak.

**Why this case is in the skill:** it is the template for the hardest honest sentence Reveal writes - *"this
is not a new idea, and that is not the end of it, because the new thing is the wedge you can prove."* The
uniqueness scorecard reads **conceptual: crowded / functional: crowded / geographical: partial / value-
proposition: open** - and it is the *value-proposition* lens, tied to documented complaints, that carries the
opportunity, not a claim of novelty.
