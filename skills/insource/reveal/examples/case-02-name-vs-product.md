# Case 2 - a name collision, a feature overlap, and zero real competition

**Idea put to Reveal:** "LedgerLamp - a personal-finance app that turns your bank transactions into a monthly
story you actually read." Global, English.
**Why this case is in the skill:** it is the worked proof of three never-list lines at once - never call an
idea duplicated because something shares its *name*, never confuse overlapping *features* with an identical
*product*, and never report *unique* just because the obvious searches came up empty. The investigation had
to resist all three temptations in a single run.

## Temptation 1 - the name collision

Search found `LedgerLamp` first as **an unrelated Etsy shop selling literal desk lamps shaped like
ledgers**, and second as **a defunct 2014 crypto portfolio tracker** with that name. A shallow pass would
file "name taken, idea taken" here. Reveal separates the axes:

- **Name dimension:** collision, real. Crypto tracker shares the name; the lamp shop shares the *word*.
- **Product similarity:** the lamp shop scores **3** (no finance, no software). The crypto tracker scores
  **41** - finance, but portfolio *tracking*, not the "monthly story" product; dead for a decade.
- **Verdict effect:** the name collision goes to a branding/trademark note in section 9. **It contributes
  nothing to the similarity of the idea.** Two facts, two places, never merged.

## Temptation 2 - the feature overlap that is not a competitor

The feature search turned up several budgeting apps - Mint-alikes, YNAB-style tools - with huge feature
overlap: transactions, categorization, charts. A feature-count ratio would score them 70-80 and the report
would say "crowded." But dimension **B** was filled from *behavior*, and dimension **I** from positioning:

- These tools are **dashboards you check**. Their whole design is glanceable numbers, budgets, and alerts.
- The proposed product is **a narrative you read** - a generated monthly story. Same raw data, a different
  job-to-be-done (understanding/meaning vs monitoring/control).
- Classified **"overlapping features, different purpose"**, scored **35-48 overall** despite 80% feature
  overlap: idea-axis and value-axis are low even though the feature-axis is high. The reasoning line is the
  lesson: *"shares the rows of the matrix, not the reason anyone opens the app."*

Had Reveal averaged the axes, the high feature score would have dragged these into "direct competitors." It
weighted idea/problem/value over raw feature count - exactly the rule in `04`.

## Temptation 3 - the empty result that is not uniqueness

Concept-axis and "personal finance monthly story" phrasings, in English, across engines and directories:
nothing identical. The lazy report line is "unique - no one does this." Reveal refused, for two reasons:

- **It had not finished discovery.** Problem-axis ("make my finances make sense to me"), adjacent-category
  ("money journaling", "financial narrative", "spending insights newsletter"), and community search surfaced
  a **write-it-down money-journaling app** and **a human "we email you your month in prose" service** -
  indirect competitors approaching the same value from different mechanisms, found only after the obvious
  concept search "came up empty."
- **Absence is interrogated, not celebrated.** The report's uniqueness section reads: *"no product found
  doing an auto-generated monthly story as its core; but (a) adjacent journaling/insight tools serve the same
  need, so the demand is real and being met imperfectly, and (b) nothing found doing it exactly can also
  mean it is a solution searching for a problem - the journaling tools' traction is the evidence the need
  exists; the absence of an automated version is an opportunity OR a signal it has been tried and failed
  (no graveyard found either way - a genuine unknown)."*

## The verdict, and what it refused to say

- **Most similar *product*:** the money-journaling app, **52** (same value, opposite workflow - manual vs
  automatic).
- **Did Reveal say "unique"?** No. It said *"no close automated implementation found across <axes,
  languages, rounds>; adjacent manual and human-powered approaches exist and have traction."* That is a
  bounded, checkable finding, not a novelty claim.
- **Did Reveal say "taken"?** No - the name collision and the feature-overlap apps were both explicitly
  *excluded* from the "your idea already exists" conclusion, each with its reasoning shown.
- **Opportunity:** real but unproven - the demand is evidenced by the adjacent tools; the automation gap is
  either the wedge or a warning, and the honest report says which way the found evidence leans (here: mildly
  toward wedge, because the adjacent tools' reviews complain the manual version is tedious).

**The one-line lesson:** Reveal's job in this case was mostly *subtraction* - refusing to let a shared name,
a shared feature row, or an empty search page each masquerade as an answer about the product. Similarity is
scored on what the thing *does for whom*, and both "taken" and "unique" must survive discovery before the
report is allowed to say either.
