# Quality Filters (Mandatory on Every Claim)

## The differentiator test

Before writing "X is an advantage of A," ask: **can B achieve the same outcome or reach the same state, even via a different mechanism?**

- If yes → not a real differentiator. Cut it, or reframe as "both do this, via different mechanisms" only when the mechanism difference itself matters (e.g. compile-time vs runtime, prescription vs OTC, genetically identical vs varied).
- If no → real differentiator. State it, then answer "so what?"

Common false-differentiator traps:
- A capability both options have, just under a different name or process.
- Traits true of both but one side's advocates emphasize more.
- Anything true of a specific variant, sub-case, or edition presented as general.
- Outdated claims or edge-case limitations that no longer hold.

See `examples/differentiator-test.md` for short worked examples across fields.

## The Trust-Me-Bro claim filter

A "trust-me-bro claim" is a superiority or magnitude claim (performance, effectiveness, size, impact) where the methodology is undisclosed, the baseline is weak/unfair, or it cannot be independently checked — yet gets repeated as settled fact.

Grade every such claim:

- **Green** — basis disclosed and/or independently corroborated or established consensus. Usable as a primary point.
- **Yellow** — single-source, self-reported, or partially disclosed. Attribute plainly ("Company X's own figures suggest…", "one small study found…") and treat as secondary.
- **Red** — undisclosed basis or clearly unfair baseline. Do not use, or explicitly flag as unverified.

Weighting rule: structure the comparison so the reader can tell primary (structural, verifiable) points apart from side points. Lead each section with the primary point; fold side points afterward with clear framing.

## Every claim needs a "why it matters"

For every bullet or row, silently check: *if I deleted the "why it matters" clause, would this line still tell the reader something they can act on or understand?* If not, add the reasoning or cut the line.

See `examples/why-it-matters.md` for before/after.

A trait name alone is never an advantage.
