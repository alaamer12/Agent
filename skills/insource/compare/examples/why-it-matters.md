# "Why it matters" — short before/after

These are deliberately drawn from different fields, so they illustrate the *pattern*, not a specific comparison to imitate.

Without reasoning (a trait name is not an advantage on its own):
> "Option B has a plugin system — this is a major feature."

With reasoning (the claim earns its place because the consequence is stated):
> "Option B has a plugin system that lets third parties add new format support without waiting on the core maintainers. Why it matters: if you need a format Option B doesn't support natively, you're not blocked waiting on a roadmap — you (or someone else) can build the extension."

Without reasoning (biology):
> "Meiosis involves crossing over — this is a key feature."

With reasoning:
> "During meiosis, homologous chromosomes exchange segments through crossing over. Why it matters: this shuffles genetic material between chromosomes before they're passed on, which is a major source of the genetic variation seen between siblings — a mechanism mitosis has no equivalent of, since mitosis is built to produce identical copies."

Without reasoning (medicine):
> "Drug A has a longer half-life than Drug B."

With reasoning:
> "Drug A has a longer half-life than Drug B (roughly 24 hours vs 6–8 hours). Why it matters: Drug A can typically be dosed once daily, which tends to improve adherence for patients who struggle with multiple daily doses — though it also means side effects take longer to clear if the patient needs to stop taking it."

The pattern to apply, regardless of domain: **trait → concrete consequence for a real task, decision, or understanding the reader has.** If the consequence can't be stated concretely, the claim probably isn't a real point yet — either dig for the actual impact or cut the line.
