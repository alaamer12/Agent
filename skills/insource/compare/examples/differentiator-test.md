# Differentiator test — short worked examples

These are deliberately drawn from different fields, so they illustrate the *test*, not a specific comparison to imitate. Substitute your own domain freely — nothing here is a fixed menu.

**Not a real differentiator (both sides achieve it, just differently):**
> Draft claim: "Option A supports reusable configuration bundles — a key advantage."
> Check: Option B has templates/macros that produce the same reusable-config outcome, just via a different mechanism.
> Verdict: cut it, or reframe as "both support reusable config, via different mechanisms" only if the mechanism itself matters (e.g. one requires a build step and the other doesn't).

**Not a real differentiator (biology):**
> Draft claim: "Mitosis produces two daughter cells — a defining trait."
> Check: meiosis also produces daughter cells (four, eventually) — cell production itself isn't the distinguishing trait.
> Verdict: cut "produces cells" as the differentiator; the real distinction is chromosome number (diploid vs haploid) and genetic identity (identical vs varied), not the bare fact that division occurs.

**A real differentiator (only one side can do it):**
> Draft claim: "Option A can run fully offline with no account; Option B requires a hosted account to function at all."
> Check: there is no equivalent workaround on Option B's side — this is a structural capability gap, not a style choice.
> Verdict: keep it, and state why it matters (e.g. "matters for anyone who needs to work without network access, or who can't send data to a third party").

**A real differentiator (biology):**
> Draft claim: "Meiosis includes crossing over and independent assortment, producing genetically varied gametes; mitosis produces genetically identical daughter cells."
> Check: mitosis has no equivalent recombination step — this is a structural, not stylistic, difference.
> Verdict: keep it — it's the core reason meiosis underlies sexual reproduction's genetic diversity while mitosis underlies growth and repair.

**A trap: edge case or specific variant mistaken for the general case:**
> Draft claim: "Option A has real-time collaboration."
> Check: real-time collaboration is only available through a third-party add-on, not the core product, and adoption of that add-on is low.
> Verdict: mention it, but scoped correctly — "available via a community add-on," not "Option A has this."

**A trap, same shape, different field (medicine):**
> Draft claim: "Treatment A has fewer side effects."
> Check: that finding comes from one small trial in a specific patient population, not a general result across populations.
> Verdict: mention it, but scoped correctly — "one study found fewer side effects in [that population]," not "Treatment A has fewer side effects" as a flat, general claim.
