# Re-validation moves

The concrete moves behind steps 1, 4, and 5 of the thaw.

## Frozen-signs catalog (step 1)

You are frozen if you hear yourself think or say:

- "The doc mandates it, so that option is out." (mechanism treated as law)
- "We already decided this." (a *draft* decision treated as closed)
- "I recommended X last turn, so X it is." (anchoring on your own output)
- "The user said X, so X is settled." (an answer treated as a mandate, not input)
- "Good enough — let's close." (stopping while a better design is reachable)
- "That conflicts with our architecture." (the architecture is itself a draft)

Name the frozen thing in one sentence before doing anything else. Naming it is half the thaw.

## Question your own pick (step 4)

1. **Steelman it.** State the strongest honest case for your current recommendation.
2. **Attack it.** What changed since you made it? What evidence arrived? What constraint did you assume
   that is actually draft? What does it cost that you downplayed?
3. **Re-derive, don't defend.** If the goal or evidence moved, re-derive from the invariant — do not
   patch the old answer to survive. A recommendation is a hypothesis, not a possession.

## Question the user's answer (step 4)

The user's reply is the best input *so far*, not a law. You may question it — respectfully, to reach
better:

- Check it still serves the goal now that the frame changed.
- Surface a trade-off they may not have weighed, or a better framing of their own intent.
- Offer the stronger option with its cost; let them decide.

This is not contrarianism. If their answer is still the best, say so and keep it. The aim is better,
not doubt performed for its own sake. **They own the call; you own the reasoning.**

## The better-still loop (step 5)

```text
best so far → new evidence / new framing → a better candidate → best so far (again)
```

Each pass targets the best reachable *now*, knowing a later pass may beat it. Do not wait for perfect;
do not stop at the first workable. "The better, even more better" is the direction, not a finish line.

## Stop rules (so thaw is not churn)

- **Close when** the decision is the best reachable with current evidence and the user has called it.
  Write it down provisionally (alternatives, trade-offs, open ends) and move on.
- **Do not reopen** a genuinely settled decision or a stable invariant without new evidence or a changed
  direction. Iteration happens across passes, not as paralysis within one.
- **Provisional closure is closure.** Marking a decision revisable is not leaving it open. The lifecycle
  continues; the topic still advances.
