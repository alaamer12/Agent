# Careers

Two honest shapes: openings exist (a list with real process), or none do (one page saying so and
where a speculative CV goes). The fabrication risk here is the job that is not open - every listed
role maps to a live requisition with a hiring owner.

## Must include - with openings

1. **Roles** - each: title, location policy ([remote] / hybrid / on-site - the real answer, ASK),
   employment type (full-time/contract - law differs), the application link that resolves.
2. **The process, stated** - steps (apply → screen → task → interview → offer), typical duration,
   whether every applicant hears back (only if true).
3. **Application privacy** - what is collected, who sees it, retention ("candidates' data kept N
   months"; the number is an ASK and lives under `privacy-policy.md`'s retention table - one
   owner, linked).
4. **Equal-opportunity line** - per jurisdiction, phrased as the law where the team actually hires
   (ASK; US/UK/EU shapes differ; a copied US boilerplate on an EU-only team is noise at best).

## Must include - no openings

One paragraph: not hiring, why a CV is still welcome (or not), where it goes and what happens to
it. This version is honest, cheap, and still answers the visitor's question.

## Writing professionally

- Team reality over employer branding: perks claimed must exist when a new hire shows up
  ("weekly learning budget" with no budget is the careers-page version of the fake audit badge).
- Roles expire: a stale listing is a broken promise on the front page - the duty to update or
  remove is part of publishing the list at all.

## Ask the user - never invent

Live openings and owners; location policy; process and response stance; jurisdiction set for the
legal lines; retention of applicant data.
