# Community Guidelines

The human-voice twin of `acceptable-use.md`: the AUP carries enforceable rules and consequences;
this page carries the culture and the tools. Both exist only where people interact (posts, chats,
names, shared content) - a single-user tool with a community page is over-engineering.

## Must include

1. **The norms, positively first** - "be the reason someone stays on this app" energy but concrete:
   respect, honest representation, constructive disagreement - 4-6 lines, each with a one-line
   example of the behavior it describes.
2. **The hard lines** - what is never acceptable HERE (harassment, hate, spam, impersonation),
   each one line, then the pointer: "the enforceable rules and consequences live in the
   Acceptable Use Policy" - the list stays owned by the AUP, this page mirrors categories not text.
3. **How to act on problems** - report flow (where the button lives, what reviewers need), and
   self-defense tools (mute, block, restrict replies) explained like features.
4. **What moderation does** - automated + human review, outcomes (removal, restriction, suspension
   - consequences owned by AUP), whether the author is notified, and the appeal path (the same
   one, linked).
5. **Special spaces** [if UGC by communities/servers/DMs] - who sets local rules beneath the
   global ones.

## Writing professionally

- Voice: the product's own, warm and specific - guidelines read like the best support doc the
  service has, not law; the ToS/AUP already exist for law.
- Every rule grounded in the product's real problem set (mod reports, support tickets); a copied
  Discord policy on a recipes app describes a community that does not exist.

## Ask the user - never invent

Which interaction surfaces exist; what has actually gone wrong before; report-to-outcome real
flow; who moderates and how fast; local-rule mechanics if any.
