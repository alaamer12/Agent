# Acceptable Use Policy (violations and what they lead to)

The violation canon: what may not be done with the service, and exactly what each violation leads
to. The ToS references this list instead of carrying its own copy - one owner per fact.

## Must include

1. **Scope** - who is bound (users, account holders whose members post, API consumers).
2. **The rules, grouped by who they protect, not by legal chapter:**
   - *Illegal*: anything unlawful where the user and the victims are.
   - *The service*: no abusing, overloading, scraping beyond limits, probing (`security.md` is
     the friendly path for that), reselling access.
   - *Other people*: spam, harassment, impersonation (`anti-fraud.md` link), hateful/harmed
     content, infringing uploads (`takedown.md`).
   - *The platform's face* [if applicable]: restrictions on white-labeling or misrepresenting.
3. **Detection and reporting** - how violations get found (automated + user reports) and the
   report route (support/contact).
4. **THE ENFORCEMENT LADDER - the user's named requirement:** for each class of violation, the
   consequence, and for the ladder, the trigger:
   [warning] → [feature limited] → [content removed] → [account suspended] → [termination].
   State what skips rungs (obvious illegality = direct termination - say it plainly), whether
   suspensions stop billing (the honest answer is usually yes; the user decides), and what
   "suspended" means concretely (read-only? no login? data kept?).
5. **Appeal** - the route and the response expectation; an enforcement document without an appeal
   line is a decree, not a policy.
6. **Transparency** [if the product has scale/UGC] - whether enforcement stats get published.

## Writing professionally

- Each rule: the act, not the mood. "You may not scrape content beyond N requests/minute or
  resell access" beats "no misuse".
- Every consequence appears twice on purpose: what breaks AND what follows - the pair is the point
  of this document; enforcement surprises are what erode trust.
- Tone: firm and readable, not threatening; the AUP is quoted by support, so write lines support
  would be comfortable sending.

## Ask the user - never invent

Which behaviors the product actually suffers/foresees (a chat app and a storage app have different
lists); the ladder's rungs and what suspension means; billing during suspension; appeal channel;
auto-detection methods (a false claim about "we monitor" is a privacy lie AND an enforcement lie).

## Failure patterns

A copy of someone's cloud-hosting AUP on a consumer chat product (half the rules describe a
service nobody sells here); "we may terminate at any time for any reason" as the entire policy
(true in some contracts, but this document exists to replace "any reason" with reasons);
violation list living in ToS too - two owners.
