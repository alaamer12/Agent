# Cancellation & Right of Withdrawal

How a customer gets *out* — the withdrawal right, the cancel flow, and what happens after.
Distinct from the Refund Policy (which answers "what happens to my money" after cancellation is
exercised): this document owns the exit. Needed by any subscription or auto-renewal product, and by
any consumer distance sale into the EU/UK; a one-time, non-EU checkout can fold it into Refund as
one section.

## Must include

1. **The right, named, with its window and clock start** — EU/UK distance contracts: 14 days from
   contract conclusion (services) or receipt of last goods (CRD 2011/83/EU Art. 9-10); US: no
   general withdrawal right — the auto-renewal statutes (ROSCA, CA ARL as amended by AB 2863
   (eff. Jul 2025), NY GBL 5-27-a) govern the cancel mechanic instead. State which frame customers
   are under (ASK).
2. **A working control on this page** — cancel here, not "email us". EU (from 19 Jun 2026, Dir.
   (EU) 2023/2673 Art. 11a) and the US state laws both trend to: the interface itself must carry a
   two-stage withdrawal/cancel action plus confirmation. A cancellation instruction ending in an
   address is the failure these rules target.
3. **Confirmation on a durable medium** — dated acknowledgement (content, date, time) sent to the
   customer immediately after the click.
4. **What cancellation means concretely** — effective date, access until period end, auto-renew
   stopped, early-termination fees (list or say "none"), and what happens to the customer's data
   and its export window (link `delete-account.md` mechanics; one owner per fact).
5. **The digital-content exception** [EU] — immediate access can consume the withdrawal right, but
   only with separate express consent + a written acknowledgement of the loss, captured at
   checkout; after CJEU C-234/25 (2025) a *streaming/subscription-type* service cannot rely on the
   waiver — the right survives until full performance. Verify per product shape, never template it.
6. **Exclusions** — where no withdrawal right exists (sealed goods unsealed, customised digital,
   price-certain services fully performed), the circumstance is listed, per CRD Art. 16, not
   asserted as "all sales final".

## Writing professionally

- The reader is leaving, not shopping: first line = "You can cancel anytime here; this is what
  happens next." No save-offer maze before the button — mandatory surveys and chained retention
  offers are documented dark patterns (CMA guidance; FTC ROSCA cases).
- Cancel must be reachable in the channel of purchase: in-app subscriptions cancel in-app (store
  flows: Google Play Subscription Center / Apple's account route), the page states both routes
  truthfully.

## Ask the user - never invent

Which markets customers buy from; window and clock-start per product kind; whether the checkout
captures the digital-content waiver (and whether it survives C-234/25 for this product); the
cancel-flow's real behavior (open it, run it); data retention after cancel; termination fees.

## Failure patterns

A refund page that never says how to stop the next charge; signup in-app but cancel only by phone
(the ROSCA "simple cancellation" theory and UK DMCCA subscription rules, in force path from 1 Jan
2027, both target exactly this); claiming the Art. 16(m) waiver for an ongoing streaming-style
service; "all sales final" shown to EU customers; no acknowledgement receipt; the page promising a
button the checkout does not have.
