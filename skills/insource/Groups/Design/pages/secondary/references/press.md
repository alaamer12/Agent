# Press / Media kit

For journalists and partners: what the product is, who runs it, the numbers, the logos. The page
exists only when the assets exist - the fabricated kit (fake logos, "brand assets coming soon",
made-up metrics) is this catalog's clearest over-engineering example: skip the page until it can
be true.

## Must include

1. **The fact sheet** - what the product is (the `about.md` sentence, reused by link not copy),
   launch date, operating entity (config-sourced), key numbers ONLY with operator confirmation
   (users, content, markets) - a press page's numbers get quoted, a wrong one becomes the quote.
2. **Brand assets** - logo files that actually download at the listed paths; real sizes; usage
   rules ("show it unmodified, credit [name], don't imply endorsement"). Missing files are dead
   links; dead links are broken promises with a brand sheet attached.
3. **Media contact** - one named route from `contact.md`'s config, with the response window the
   team will keep (publishers work on deadlines; "we reply within 3 days" when nobody owns press
   is the fastest way to lose coverage).
4. **Screen imagery** - actual current screenshots, captioned with what they show; yesterday's UI
   gets quoted as today's.
5. **Statements** - the "about [product]" boilerplate paragraph a journalist can paste verbatim;
   the operator approves its wording (ASK - this is the product's voice, not the skill's).

## Writing professionally

- Press pages serve the person on deadline: copy-paste ready, short, dated, no adjectives that
  require proof.
- Update duty: every number or screenshot has the date it was true; changes to facts here edit
  this page in the same change (`agents-md` §15 style - here as the operator's habit).

## Ask the user - never invent

Whether media interest exists at all; real numbers; who owns press; the approved statement; the
actual asset files. "No press page yet" is a perfect outcome.
