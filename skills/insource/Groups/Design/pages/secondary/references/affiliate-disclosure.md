# Affiliate & Endorsement Disclosure

The material-connection page: what partnerships pay you, how content gets sponsored, and the
review/testimonial policy. Gate: affiliate programs, gifted product, influencer/UGC campaigns, or
sponsored posts — one or more means this exists; a product site with zero endorsement economics
does not need it (and a page nobody's claims touch is theatre).

## Must include

1. **The definition and the promise** — what counts as a material connection (commission, payment,
   gifted product, employment, family tie, contest entry), and that every one gets disclosed
   **per-claim, adjacent to the claim** — the page never substitutes for in-content disclosure
   (FTC and ASA both reject bio-only).
2. **Programs, named** — "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" verbatim where
   Amazon's agreement applies; each network named; commissions stated; "price is the same either
   way".
3. **Sponsored vs editorial vs product placement** — each labeled; placement alone is not a
   disclosure; advertorial headlines say sponsored.
4. **Review/testimonial policy** — real experience only; no paid-positive; no suppressing negative
   reviews (US Consumer Review Rule, 16 CFR 465, live Oct 2024 — civil penalties per violation);
   insider/employee reviews labeled; atypical results paired with what is generally expected.
5. **How disclosures appear** — "Ad"/"#ad"/"Paid link" words (FTC rejected "affiliate link" alone
   as label next to ambiguous text; ASA rejected "sponsored"/"gifted"/"in association with");
   upfront not after "see more"; same language as the content; per device.
6. **Ad networks and personalization** — third-party units and cookie-based personalization
   disclosed, with the `cookie-policy.md` link.
7. **Correction route + effective date.**

## Writing professionally

- The advertiser is liable for its endorsers: if the project runs an influencer program, the page
  states the monitoring/remedy duty, because the contract chain is the reader's evidence.
- Disclosure words match across every surface (post, caption, story, video, PDF) — one vocabulary,
  stated once here, applied everywhere.

## Ask the user - never invent

Which programs actually run; who creates content (staff/creators/users); the review-selection
process; platform declarations already in use (YouTube paid-promo toggle, Meta branded content).

## Failure patterns

A disclosure page while individual posts stay silent (the page is the policy, not the disclosure);
English disclosure on Spanish content; one disclosure assumed to cover an ongoing relationship;
forgetting employee reviews; "#sweeps" instead of a brand-specific tag on giveaways (cross-ref
`promotions-rules.md`).
