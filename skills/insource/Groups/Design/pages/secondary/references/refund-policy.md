# Refund Policy

The money-expectation document: what gets money back, by when, how, and what never does.

## Must include

1. **Product-type split, stated first** - digital subscriptions, one-time digital purchases, and
   physical goods have different physics and different consumer-law frames; one section each, and
   sections stay out when the project does not sell that kind.
2. **The window** - per kind: N days from what date (purchase? activation? delivery? - all three
   are different policies).
3. **The withdrawal right** [EU/UK consumer contracts] - the statutory 14 days plus the
   digital-content exception (immediate access + explicit waiver + acknowledgment, which the
   checkout must implement to be true); which jurisdictions the product's customers are in is an
   ASK, not a template field.
4. **How to request** - channel, what evidence helps, one-click where the product can.
5. **Processing time** - "reviewed within N, returned within M" - both real numbers (ASK).
6. **Non-refundables, explicit** - consumed usage, prorated periods, sale items, the list with
   reasons.
7. **Goodwill stance** [optional, recommended] - "outside the rules, talk to us" - only if the
   support team can honor it.

## Writing professionally

- The reader arrives annoyed: first line answers "can I get my money back, how fast" before any
  condition clause.
- Cross-check every number against the payment stack's real behavior and the ToS pointer - a
  refund promise the billing system cannot execute is the failure.

## Ask the user - never invent

Product kinds sold; windows per kind; jurisdictions of customers; channels; processing times;
non-refundable list; chargeback stance.

## Failure patterns

30-day promise beside a payment processor whose refund API caps at the provider window; "all sales
final" on a subscription product (contradicts its own cancellation section); silent EU section for
an EU-selling app.
