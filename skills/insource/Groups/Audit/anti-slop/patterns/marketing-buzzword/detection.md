# Detection: Generic marketing claims

## Catalog route

The official catalog classifies this as a **source** rule. Phrase lists can locate candidates, but context, offer, audience, and claim evidence determine whether copy is vague or unsupported.

## Candidate signals

1. Review headlines/taglines/CTAs for abstract promises and superlatives.
2. Check whether surrounding content explains product, audience, concrete outcome, comparison scope, and evidence.
3. For objective/implied performance claims, record the reasonable consumer interpretation and supporting substantiation.
4. Evaluate full-page copy rather than isolated words.

## False-positive controls

- Do not flag a phrase solely because it appears on a buzzword list.
- Do not treat subjective slogan/puffery as equivalent to a factual measurable claim.
- Do not infer deception or AI authorship from generic phrasing alone.
- Do not remove concise positioning if nearby copy makes the offer clear.

## Test disposition

Source candidates may identify phrases like generic superlatives, but editorial review must assess specificity, context, and substantiation. Include a concise valid slogan and an unsupported claim as negative/positive examples. Regex cannot confirm.
