# Cream / beige palette

**ID:** `cream-palette`  
**Catalog section:** Color & Contrast  
**Official modality:** Source

## Definition and empirical evidence

The catalog flags cream/beige as a possible reflexive “tasteful” default, while explicitly allowing these colors when they belong to the product. Warm neutrals can be a deliberate foundation; novelty is not itself a design goal.

## Underlying principle and potential harm

Choose colors for brand, audience, content, user needs, and clear interface roles. An unconsidered neutral palette can make unrelated products feel interchangeable or underdevelop hierarchy/brand character. Pale text and controls may also be hard to distinguish, but WCAG evaluates the rendered pair—not whether its colors are neutral or fashionable.

## Recognition signals

- The same cream surface and tan/terracotta accents are applied regardless of product, content, or role.
- Palette values do not distinguish surfaces, actions, or states in a useful way.
- Small text/muted labels lose contrast against warm surfaces.
- Repeated warm-neutral values and shared tokens are source clues; they cannot prove intent, sameness, or accessibility.

## Causes to investigate

Possible causes include adopting a fashionable editorial style without checking brand fit; retaining a starter-template palette; choosing colors before defining roles; or relying on cream/beige alone to imply “natural” or premium.

## Exceptions and false positives

- Editorial brands may use a restrained palette to keep attention on content, photography, or products.
- Cream/beige can suit hospitality, natural-product, tactile, or other identities when backed by the actual brand/audience.
- Context-specific studies show some audiences prefer neutrals in certain settings; this does not generalize to all products/users.
- Beige can be coherent and useful; a novel palette can still be generic or mismatched.

## Correction guidance

Confirm brand, audience, and content context. If the palette is an unconsidered default, revise only where it fails to express that intent or obscures useful hierarchy; select colors for clear roles rather than novelty. If warm neutrals fit, retain them and make the rest of the palette equally deliberate. Test rendered text contrast across applicable states/themes.
