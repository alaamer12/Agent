# Em-dash overuse

**ID:** `em-dash-overuse`  
**Catalog section:** Copy  
**Official modality:** Source

## Definition and empirical evidence

An em dash can mark interruption, emphatic pause, or a clarifying/parenthetical thought; it overlaps with commas, parentheses, colons, semicolons, and sentence breaks but conveys different emphasis. Editorial conventions vary, and “overuse” is contextual—there is no universal count or punctuation-based authorship test.

## Underlying principle and potential harm

Use punctuation when it clarifies meaning/rhythm and keeps sentences easy to follow. Repeated interruptions can make prose choppy, overly emphatic, or hard to parse, especially in long sentences with multiple clauses/asides. This is an editorial risk, not an inherent property of the mark.

## Recognition signals

- Several unrelated interruptions/asides cluster within a short passage.
- Dashes substitute for clear sentence boundaries or connect clauses without making the relationship easy to follow.
- Reading aloud reveals choppy rhythm or repeated emphasis that adds no distinction.
- Frequency/count searches locate review candidates but cannot show whether a dash is unnecessary or violates house style.

## Causes to investigate

Possible explanations include deliberate voice/rhythm; emphatic asides; sentences accumulating clauses in drafting; or house-style convention. A repeated pattern may come from human writing, AI-assisted editing, or generated text; punctuation alone cannot distinguish authorship.

## Exceptions and false positives

- A dash can clearly set off a phrase containing commas, mark interruption, or create stronger emphasis than parentheses.
- A recurring dash style may fit the author/genre while remaining clear.
- Style guides vary: AP states no fixed maximum but advises restraint; Chicago describes some uses as taste/reader effect.
- AP rejects em-dash frequency as an AI tell; authorship needs evidence beyond one punctuation habit.

## Correction guidance

Edit for meaning and rhythm, not a quota. Keep dashes that clarify or intentionally emphasize; replace only those that interrupt without adding value. Consider commas, parentheses, colons, semicolons, conjunctions, or sentence breaks according to meaning. Split overlong sentences when that aids comprehension and apply house spacing/punctuation consistently. Do not infer AI authorship from dash frequency.
