# Worked example: Normal vs. Extreme mode on the same source

## Source (~150 words)

> The Q3 retention review found that overall customer churn rose from 6.1% to 8.4% quarter over quarter. Initially, the team suspected the August outage was the primary driver, since the outage generated a spike in support tickets and negative social sentiment during that week. However, a deeper cohort analysis showed that churn was concentrated almost entirely among customers on the mid-tier "Growth" plan, which received a price increase in July — a month before the outage. Customers on the Basic and Enterprise plans showed churn consistent with prior quarters. The team recommends reversing or phasing in the Growth-tier price increase more gradually, and notes that this conclusion is based on one quarter of data; a second quarter of observation is recommended before making the pricing change permanent.

## Normal mode (executive summary register)

**Bottom line:** Q3's churn increase was driven by the July price increase on the Growth-tier plan, not the August outage as initially assumed — the team recommends reversing or phasing in that increase more gradually.

Churn rose from 6.1% to 8.4% quarter over quarter, but cohort analysis showed it was concentrated almost entirely among Growth-tier customers, while Basic and Enterprise churn stayed in line with prior quarters. Since the price increase preceded the outage by a month, pricing — not the outage — is the more likely cause. The team flags this as based on one quarter of data and recommends a second quarter of observation before making the pricing change permanent.

## Extreme mode

```
- **Finding:** Q3 churn rose 6.1% → 8.4%, driven by the Growth-tier price increase, not the August outage.
- **Evidence:** churn concentrated in Growth tier; Basic/Enterprise unaffected; price increase preceded outage by a month.
- **Recommendation:** reverse or phase in the Growth-tier increase more gradually.
- **Caveat:** based on one quarter; second quarter of data recommended before making permanent.
```

## What to notice

- Both modes lead with the same bottom line (the price increase, not the outage) — mode never changes *what* the point is, only how much surrounds it.
- The caveat about one quarter of data survives in both modes, because it's must-keep: dropping it would make the recommendation sound more settled than the source claims.
- Extreme mode drops the initial-suspicion detail (outage → support tickets → sentiment) entirely, since it's should-keep color explaining how the team got there, not part of the finding itself.
- Neither mode invents a number, date, or figure not in the source.
