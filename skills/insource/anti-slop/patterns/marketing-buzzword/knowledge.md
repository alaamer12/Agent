# Generic marketing claims

**ID:** `marketing-buzzword`  
**Catalog section:** Copy  
**Official modality:** Source

## Definition and empirical evidence

The catalog flags broad, high-promise phrases such as “supercharge” or “world-class” that do not explain what the product does or what improves for the user. Its principle is to say what people can do and what benefit follows. A generic-sounding phrase alone does not establish deception or AI authorship.

## Underlying principle and potential harm

Make the offering and user value understandable to the intended audience. Vague copy may leave people unsure what is offered, whether it fits their needs, or how it differs from alternatives. Objective or implied claims of measurable benefit may mislead if unsupported; a phrase’s meaning depends on context and reasonable interpretation.

## Recognition signals

- Abstract verbs or superlatives could describe almost any product and leave the offer unexplained.
- Relative claims such as “better” or “more powerful” lack a clear comparison/scope.
- A headline promises performance/savings/safety without nearby evidence or qualification.
- The audience cannot identify what the product does, who benefits, or what outcome is intended from headline/supporting copy/CTA together.

## Causes to investigate

Possible causes include writing before offer/audience are defined; using category language instead of concrete outcomes; unsupported superlatives; or reusing a brand slogan where users need a product description.

## Exceptions and false positives

- A concise slogan can provide distinctive positioning if nearby text explains the offer.
- Subjective opinion/puffery differs from an objective or implied claim that needs substantiation; context matters.
- Campaign/brand copy may be intentionally evocative where context still supplies meaning.
- Generic phrasing alone proves neither deception nor AI generation; an accurate objective claim may also be concise.

## Correction guidance

Start with the audience’s task/need, then state the relevant product/service, concrete benefit, and meaningful differentiator in plain language. Keep a slogan if it contributes positioning; add an explanatory line if the offer remains unclear. Identify reasonable interpretations of factual/implied performance claims and substantiate them before publication; qualify limited scope clearly. Test understanding with representative users rather than optimizing for novelty or deleting every broad phrase.
